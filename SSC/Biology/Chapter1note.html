Biology: The Basics

What is Biology?

Derived from Greek words Bios (Life) and Logos (Knowledge). It is the science of living beings.

Two main original branches:

  • Botany: Discussion of plants.
  • Zoology: Discussion of animals.

Classification System

Characteristics of the 5 Kingdoms

1. Monera

  • Basically unicellular, filamentous, or colonial.
  • Prokaryotic: Chromatin present but no nuclear membrane.
  • No membrane-bound organelles (mitochondria, plastids absent).
  • Reproduction mainly by binary fission.
  • Nutrition: Autotrophic (photosynthesis) or Heterotrophic.
Examples: Bacteria, Nostoc

2. Protista

  • Unicellular or multicellular colonial.
  • Eukaryotic: Well-defined nucleus (nuclear membrane present).
  • DNA wrapped with protein inside chromatin.
  • Move via pseudopodia, flagella, or cilia.
  • Reproduction: Asexual (Mitosis) or Sexual (Conjugation).
Examples: Amoeba, Diatoms

3. Fungi

  • Most are terrestrial, saprophytic, or parasitic.
  • Body consists of thread-like hyphae (mycelium).
  • Cell wall made of Chitin.
  • No chloroplasts (Heterotrophic).
  • Reproduce by haploid spores.
Examples: Yeast, Penicillium, Mushroom

4. Plantae

  • Autotrophic (photosynthetic), multicellular eukaryotes.
  • Cell wall made of Cellulose.
  • Possess complex tissue systems.
  • Reproduction: Sexual (Anisogamous) and Asexual.
  • Embryo is formed from zygote.
Examples: Artocarpus (Jackfruit), Spirogyra

5. Animalia

  • Multicellular eukaryotes with no cell wall.
  • No plastids (Heterotrophic).
  • Usually have vacuole-free cells.
  • Complex tissue systems (Nerves, Muscles).
  • Sexual reproduction (Oogamous).
Examples: Homo sapiens (Human), Panthera tigris (Tiger)

Carolus Linnaeus

Father of Taxonomy

A Swedish naturalist. In his book Systema Naturae (10th Ed, 1758), he introduced the rules of Binomial Nomenclature.

Binomial Nomenclature

The system of naming an organism with two parts: the Genus and the Species.

ICBN Rules:

  • 1. Language must be Latin.
  • 2. Two parts: Genus (starts with Capital) + Species (small letter).
  • 3. Must be unique for every organism.
  • 4. In print: Italicized.
  • 5. Handwritten: Underlined separately.
Genus + species = Homo sapiens
